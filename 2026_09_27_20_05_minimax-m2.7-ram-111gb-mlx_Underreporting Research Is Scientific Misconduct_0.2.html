Underreporting Research Is Scientific Misconduct
  • Source: JAMA, March 9, 1990 - Vol 263, No. 10
  • Topic: Publication bias and selective reporting in clinical trials
  • Author(s): Iain Chalmers, FRCOG
Definition and Scope of Scientific Misconduct
  • Two forms:
  • Sins of commission --> deliberate falsification of data
  • Sins of omission --> may be even more important
  • Selective underreporting is more widespread and more harmful than falsification
  • No protective mechanism exists for detecting selective underreporting
Extent of Underreporting Problem
  • ~1 in 2 trials reported in summary form never --> full publication allowing informed judgment
  • Unpublished to published trial ratio: 1:128 (1940–1984 survey)
  • Focused surveys suggest ratio closer to 1:5
  • Manual searches yield --> ~2x more trials than MEDLINE retrieval
Consequences of Underreporting Clinical Trials
Ovarian Cancer Chemotherapy Example (Simes)
  • Published trials suggested --> combination chemotherapy preferred over single alkylating agent
  • Analysis of registered trials (results unknown beforehand) failed to support this preference
Twin Pregnancy Bed Rest Trial (Harare, 1977)
  • First randomized evaluation of routine hospitalization for uncomplicated twin pregnancy
  • Results showed --> ↑preterm delivery risk with routine hospitalization
  • Policy abandoned in Harare based on trial findings
Delayed Publication Consequences
  • Trial results remained unpublished for 7 years
  • Two visitors "discovered" unpublished data and helped investigators publish
  • Full account published in The Lancet only after this discovery
Impact on Clinical Practice
  • Delayed reevaluation of policy widely accepted for 4 decades
  • Led to unnecessary resource deployment
  • May have caused continued use of harmful policy
Routine Ultrasonography Debate
  • Published trials show --> routine ultrasonography ↓ induction for "postterm" pregnancies
  • Insufficient data to assess effects on substantive adverse outcomes
Unpublished Large Trial (1980s)
  • One large trial remains unpublished
  • Results could inform current policy either way
Inadequate Reporting Example (Eik-Nes Trial, Norway 1979–1980)
  • Only published report: letter to The Lancet
  • Contradictory accounts of control group:
  • Letter: controls could be referred for ultrasound on clinical indication
  • NIH report: controls had no ultrasound at all
  • Full publication never occurred
Difficulties in Correcting Underreporting
Identifying Unpublished Trials
  • Survey of >40,000 obstetricians and pediatricians across 18 countries
  • Only 18 unpublished trials identified (1940–1984)
  • At least 2,300 reports of perinatal trials published during same period
  • Ratio suggests --> substantial numbers of unpublished trials not identified
Reducing Reporting Biases
  • Contacted investigators to obtain missing information
  • Published in book form (Effective Care in Pregnancy and Childbirth)
  • Created --> Oxford Database of Perinatal Trials (continuously updated electronic publication)
Limitations
  • Cannot know if precautions succeeded in producing unbiased estimates
  • Systematic manual searches of ~60 core journals back to 1950
  • Methodological descriptors in MEDLINE --> underretrieve relevant trials
Solutions: Who Must Change Behavior
Clinical Investigators
  • Primary responsibility for ensuring full study reporting
  • Heads of departments must --> ultimate responsibility if short-term contracts compromise completion
Research Ethics Committees
  • Currently only doing half their job
  • Must assess whether approved research was --> conducted as agreed AND reported appropriately
Actions Recommended
  • Publish regular reports identifying studies that received committee endorsement
  • Establish mechanisms for monitoring and recording investigators' compliance
Funding Bodies
  • Should make award of funds conditional on full publication
  • Surprisingly common that this is not current practice
Journal Editors
  • Must purge practices of dichotomizing reports into "positive" vs "negative" results
  • Accept or reject based on --> well conceptualized and competently executed
  • Not based on direction or magnitude of differences
Pediatrics Editor Initiative
  • Willing to arrange review of trial protocols for provisional acceptance upon successful completion
Commercial Research Collaborations
  • Investigators must ensure results analyzed/reported by people without commercial vested interest
  • Better decisions possible if expectations made explicit at outset
Prospective Trial Registration: The Solution
What It Enables
  • Detection of selective underreporting by comparing:
  • Inferences from trials registered before results known
  • Inferences from all available trials (registered and unregistered)
  • If conclusions conflict --> selective underreporting suspected
  • Prefer inferences from trials registered at inception (if methodologically acceptable)
Advantages of Registration
  • Greater confidence in assessing validity of brief reports (methods publicly available)
  • Addresses suspicion of selective reporting of certain outcomes
  • Allows consultation of protocol to verify --> which data items recorded and outcomes specified
Existing Registries
  • National Cancer Institute
  • National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases
  • National Institute of Dental Research
  • British Medical Research Council
  • European Organization for the Research and Treatment of Cancer
  • International Committee on Thrombosis and Haemostasis
  • Oxford Database of Perinatal Trials
How Registries Are Populated
  • Contacts with investigators and department heads
  • Research ethics committees
  • Funding bodies
  • Journal editors
Implementation Requirements
  • National and international funding bodies must --> provide leadership, organization, and funding
  • National Library of Medicine should coordinate activity
  • Investigators and research ethics committees must play respective roles
Role for Journal Editors
  • Indicate that registration of controlled trials at inception --> evidence of scientific good conduct
  • Encourage these developments through acceptance criteria
Electronic Publishing as Solution to Space Limitations
  • Shortage of space in printed journals --> no longer valid excuse
  • Medical publishing must exploit electronic potential
  • Proposed solution:
  • Publish structured abstracts on paper
  • Full reports published electronically
Ethical and Practical Imperatives
Why Adequate Reporting Is Required
  • Scientific reasons: enable judgments about validity of results
  • Ethical reasons: patients who participated deserve contribution to knowledge
Consequences of Inadequate Reporting
  • Patients may receive --> ineffective or dangerous forms of care
  • May delay recognition that other forms of care are beneficial
  • Does injustice to patients who participated in trials
  • Does injustice to collaborators and those who provided resources
Simes' Contribution
  • Demonstrated that prospective registration makes it possible to:
  • Seek selective underreporting
  • Detect selective underreporting
Key Statistics Summary
  • 50% of trials never reported in sufficient detail after summary publication
  • Ratio unpublished:published = 1:128 (large survey)
  • Ratio unpublished:published ≈ 1:5 (focused surveys)
  • Manual searches retrieve --> 2x more trials than MEDLINE
  • Harare trial unpublished for 7 years
  • Twin pregnancy bed rest policy unchallenged for 4 decades
