AI In Your Endoscopy Practice: How and What Should You Be Using
  • Source: Gastroenterology Conference Session
  • Topic: Artificial Intelligence Applications in Endoscopy Practice
  • Author(s): Lejert C, Silverman A, Sharma P, Parasat S (et al.)
1. AI Impact in Healthcare
  • 22% of healthcare organizations have deployed commercial AI products
  • 9% adoption rate across the US economy overall
  • AI leads in healthcare dollars spent compared to other sectors
1.1 Where Healthcare AI Dollars Go
  • Ambient scribes for documentation
  • Coding and billing automation
  • Workflow optimization tasks
2. AI Capabilities in Endoscopy
2.1 What AI Enables
  • Better detection and diagnosis through computer-aided detection (CADe)
  • Decision support for clinical decision-making
  • Efficiency improvements in workflow
  • Personalized care delivery to patients
  • Standardization of quality and type of care provided
2.2 Risks and Disadvantages of AI
  • Bias risk: If training data contains bias, algorithm reinforces existing disparities
  • Data shift: Algorithm trained on specific population → performance degrades when used on different populations
  • Overreliance: Loss of clinical skills when blindly trusting AI
  • Workflow distortion: Alert fatigue from constant AI reminders
  • Accountability issues: Unclear who is responsible when AI makes diagnosis
3. Colon Polyp Detection (CADe)
3.1 FDA/Europe Approved Products
  • 5 products approved for colon polyp detection (as of presentation)
  • Colon polyp detection has been the starting point for AI development in endoscopy
3.2 Evidence Base
  • ~48 randomized controlled studies with over 40,000 patients
  • Largest evidence base for any AI application in endoscopy
3.3 Clinical Impact from Studies
  • Normal detection rate ↑ 20%
  • Adenoma miss rate ↓ approximately 50%
  • Adenomas per colonoscopy ↑ significantly
  • Benefits apply to all providers regardless of experience level (not just low ADR performers)
  • No significant impact on withdrawal time
3.4 Caveats and Limitations
  • Algorithms detect more diminutive polyps → primary driver of ↑ ADR
  • Real-world performance gains are smaller than RCTs demonstrate
  • Risk of descaling (losing ability to detect without AI assistance)
  • Alert fatigue from false alarms
4. Colorectal Polyp Diagnosis (CADx)
4.1 Optical Biopsy Capability
  • Algorithms determine polyp histology in real-time
  • Accuracy matches experts: approximately 85–90%
4.2 Performance Variability Factors
  • Product location differences
  • Image modality variations
  • Operator experience levels
4.3 Clinical Application Requirements
  • Must visualize the polyp → algorithm provides diagnosis
  • Operator must agree/disagree with confidence level
  • Enables resect and discard or diagnose and leave strategies for diminutive polyps
4.4 Approved Products (Europe/FDA Pipeline)
  • Multiple products undergoing FDA approval
  • European approvals already in place for several solutions
4.5 Advantages of CADx Implementation
  • Immediate decision on adenoma vs hyperplastic polyp
  • Hyperplastic polyps → can be left behind
  • High accuracy in clinical practice
  • Cost savings on pathology and equipment
  • ↓ unnecessary polypectomies and associated risks
  • Faster workflow: patients can be told surveillance interval same-day without waiting for histopathology
4.6 Disadvantages and Concerns
  • Performance inconsistent under different circumstances
  • Image quality is critical factor for accuracy
  • Risk of misclassification leading to missed adenomas
  • Risk of overreliance when blindly trusting AI diagnosis
  • Need for ongoing training with and without AI
5. Why Lesions Are Missed
  • Hard to see lesions
  • Technique, time, and fatigue issues
  • Lack of mucosal exposure (poor bowel prep)
  • Not recognized → inattentional blindness phenomenon
  • Lesion visible but not consciously registered
5.1 Barrett's Esophagus Example
  • Lesions relatively uncommon → easily overlooked
  • General endoscopists lack familiarity with presentation
  • AI can help detect subtle abnormalities
6. Barrett's Esophagus AI (Emerging)
6.1 Current Status
  • Not yet approved but in approval pipeline (next few years)
  • Demonstrates expert-level performance in control settings
6.2 Algorithm Features
  • Bounding box or segmentation algorithms
  • Highlights abnormality location in Barrett's segment
  • Real-time detection of subtle lesions
6.3 Future Potential
  • May replace need for random biopsies
  • Could transform Barrett's surveillance practice
7. Capsule Endoscopy AI
7.1 The Problem
  • Long video review → reader fatigue
  • Inefficiency in traditional reading
7.2 AI Solution Benefits
  • Captures abnormal frames automatically
  • Shortens reading time to <10 minutes
  • Preserves or improves lesion detection
  • ↑ consistency (less reader fatigability)
  • Scalable: cloud-based reads without on-premise compute
7.3 Products Available
  • Several products currently available or in development
  • Multiple companies developing solutions
7.4 Caveats
  • Performance variability across platforms
  • Detecting lesions of unclear clinical significance
  • Risk of overreliance → AI serves as second reader, not autonomous reader
8. Quality Metrics in AI-Assisted Endoscopy
8.1 Foundation Requirement
  • High-quality endoscopy is essential for optimal AI performance
  • Quality metrics are the cornerstone of all AI applications
8.2 Indirect vs Direct Quality Measurement
  • Anomaly detection rate → indirect quality measure
  • Both passive and active measurement approaches exist
8.3 Future Real-Time Capabilities (Emerging)
  • Mucosal coverage assessment
  • Blind spot detection
  • Bowel prep scoring
  • Withdrawal time tracking
  • Landmark recognition
  • Cecal intubation confirmation
8.4 Validation Evidence
  • Early study: prospectively validated 110 colonoscopy videos
  • Quality algorithm measured:
  • Withdrawal time
  • Cecal intubation time
  • Bowel prep scoring
  • High agreement with experts for withdrawal and cecal intubation
  • Bowel prep scoring still needs further development
9. AI-Assisted Reporting: Core Technologies
9.1 Natural Language Processing (NLP) & LLMs
  • Interprets free-text dictation
  • Maps to structured clinical data
  • Generates coherent, contextually appropriate report narratives
9.2 Voice Recognition / Speech-to-Text
  • Converts real-time dictation → report drafts as procedure unfolds
9.3 Computer Vision (Beyond Polyp Detection)
  • Passive detection of endoscopic tools:
  • Snares
  • Biopsy forceps
  • Clipping devices
  • Quality metrics capture:
  • Withdrawal time
  • Cecal intubation
  • Retroflexion
  • Bowel prep quality scales
  • Disease activity scoring for IBD, EoE, Barrett's
9.4 Integrated Pipeline Concept
  • Audio + video data → multimodal generative AI pipeline
  • Drafts report with associated finding images
  • Designed as draft generator, not autonomous reporter
  • Endoscopist reviews, edits, and signs
  • Liability structure unchanged
10. AI-Assisted Reporting: Evidence Base
10.1 Current Status
  • Formal evaluations limited but growing
  • Studies span voice-enabled and computer vision systems
  • Covers both upper and lower endoscopy
10.2 Findings Summary
  • Efficiency gains documented but highly dependent on baseline workflow comparison
  • Quality and completeness reliably improved with AI-enabled reporting vs without
  • Clinician satisfaction is variable (largely tracks with platform quality)
11. AI-Assisted Reporting: Key Considerations
11.1 Data Privacy & Security
  • Must meet HIPAA standards for cloud platforms handling PHI
  • Verify directly with each vendor (do not assume)
  • Review how vendors handle data for monetization concerns
  • Patient data should not be used to train external models without explicit consent
11.2 Clinician Accountability
  • Signing physician remains fully responsible for every word in AI-assisted report
  • AI is a tool, not a cosigner
  • Recommend formally documenting AI involvement in report generation
11.3 Current Gaps and Limitations
  • Generalizability: Many systems trained at few beta testing sites → monitoring essential for performance in different settings
  • Oversight: AI behaves and fails differently than standard software → need purpose-built oversight systems
  • EHR Integration: Currently fragmented across platforms (but changing rapidly)
  • Business Case: Costs vary; for already efficient documenters, speed gains may be modest
12. Future of AI-Assisted Reporting
12.1 True EHR Integration Vision
  • Voice recognition + computer vision + quality capture → seamless real-time pipeline
  • Procedure to finalized report in one flow
  • Ideally includes patient-friendly summaries
12.2 Genuine Contextualization (Future State)
  • Reports draw on:
  • Patient history
  • Prior findings
  • Surveillance intervals (auto-generated with guideline citations)
  • Individualized to each patient's risk profile
12.3 Automated Quality & Research Infrastructure
  • ADR trends → automatic tracking
  • Disease activity scores → automated capture
  • Prep adequacy monitoring → background process
  • Documentation completeness checks → automatic system
13. Future-Proofing: Learning from Other Industries
13.1 Aviation Industry Model
  • Assistive control systems for pilots
  • Standardized checklists (computer-driven)
  • Black box data → continuous learning from every flight
  • Crew resource management ≠ human redundancy
  • System designed to reduce burden, not add to it
13.2 Radiology Model
  • Queue-based image processing
  • AI triages and prioritizes cases
  • Human makes final decision
  • Radiologists moved up the value chain → decision makers
  • AI handles routine; humans handle complex cases
13.3 Formula One Centaur Model
  • Human-AI collaboration for optimal performance
  • AI proposes constantly; human frames and decides
  • System learns from both machine and human feedback
  • Even 0.15 seconds matters for competitive advantage
13.4 FinTech Model
  • Continuous risk assessment in real-time
  • Multiple data streams analyzed simultaneously
  • Adaptive scheduling based on current data (not fixed schedules)
  • Personalized risk stratification for screening decisions
13.5 DevOps Continuous Improvement Model
  • Measure → review → change workflow → retrain → repeat
  • Every event returns data to entire improvement loop
  • Systematic approach to system optimization
14. Endoscopy Suite of the Future Architecture
14.1 Paradigm Shift
  • Moving from: "What device should I buy?"
  • → To: "What system should I build?"
14.2 AI Quality Improvement Model
  • AI reduces variance across all rooms and operators
  • Focus on system improvement, not just individual performance
  • More durable than targeting low-performing individuals
14.3 Hub Design Components
  • Procedure room (core)
  • AI triage hub
  • Smart scheduling system
  • Pathology integration loop
  • Data governance layer
  • Quality registry in back-end
  • Continuous quality improvement loop
14.4 Current Architectural Elements Available
  • Real-time vision algorithms (cloud-based)
  • Quality improvement tools
  • Capsule reading solutions
  • Documentation systems
  • EHR platforms
14.5 Integration Strategy Question
  • Don't ask: "Which product do I need?"
  • Ask instead: "Which layer needs strengthening?"
15. Implementation Framework
15.1 Short-Term (3 months)

  • Begin with single AI application integration
15.2 Medium-Term (1–2 years)

15.3 Long-Term (2–5 years)
  • Adaptive framework across entire system

15.4 Governance Requirements
  • Monitoring systems in place
  • Human override capability maintained
  • Regular validation of AI outputs
  • Audit trails for all AI-assisted decisions

17. Vendor Evaluation Framework
17.1 Core Questions to Ask
  • Problem fit: Does solution address a real problem you have?
  • Scalability: Can it layer additional tools as they develop?
  • Scope compatibility: Does it integrate with your equipment and EHR?
  • Standard pathways: Are there plug-and-play options (HDMI, SD ports)?
  • Regulatory status: FDA approved? What are indications for use?
  • Cost of ownership: Device + maintenance + contracts + EHR integration fees
17.2 Technical Evaluation Points
  • Edge vs cloud: Is algorithm on-premise or cloud-based?
  • Latency acceptable for real-time use
  • Regulatory evidence: Is FDA approval needed or sufficient?
  • Liability framework considerations
17.3 Data Ownership Clarity
  • Who owns patient data?
  • Hospital vs physician vs third party
  • Critical for future research and revenue streams
17.4 UI/UX Considerations
  • Bounding box vs segmentation mask vs explainability model
  • Probability display preferences (e.g., 85% confidence)
  • Control interface options (foot pedal, voice commands)
18. Documentation AI: Practical Considerations
18.1 NLP Limitations (Current State)
  • NLP can extract information but not perfectly
  • More reliable for structured data extraction
  • Prioritize: ICD codes, billing information
18.2 EHR Integration Requirements
  • Bidirectional communication is essential
  • Avoid systems that only push data one way
  • Must enable future audits and QI projects
18.3 Voice & Editing Capabilities
  • Voice capture for dictation
  • Manual editing flexibility maintained
19. Staff Training Framework (4-Week Plan)
19.1 Super User Development
  • Select staff members to become experts on AI tools
  • Deep understanding of system capabilities and limitations
19.2 Ongoing Monitoring
  • Regular audits of AI tool performance
  • Feedback loops with frontline staff
19.3 Team Training Components
  • Nurses and technicians included in training
  • All staff understand workflow integration
20. Key Implementation Takeaways
20.1 Strategy Sequence
  • Pick the problem first, then select vendor
  • Ensure vendor can scale to your needs
  • Integration is a project, not just installation
20.2 Technical Requirements
  • AI is one component → integration requires broader project management
  • Architecture planning essential for future-proofing
20.3 Training Imperatives
  • Train the entire team (nurses, technicians, physicians)
  • Build feedback loops where staff report issues
  • Measure outcomes with and without AI
20.4 Continuous Improvement
  • Renegotiate vendor relationships as needed
  • Reassess tool effectiveness regularly
  • Stay current with evolving capabilities
21. AI Documentation: Override and Uncertainty Handling
21.1 When Endoscopist Disagrees with AI
  • All products allow manual override of any component
  • This functionality is standard across platforms
21.2 AI Uncertainty Scenarios
  • When endoscopist unsure AND AI provides confident diagnosis
  • Confidence level display (e.g., 50% vs 85%) helps inform judgment
  • Human judgment ultimately prevails regardless of AI confidence
21.3 Future Research Needs
  • How does AI affect clinician uncertainty?
  • Impact on decision-making when AI provides confident vs uncertain outputs
  • Need for more research on optimal human-AI collaboration
22. Summary: Future-Proofing Principles
22.1 Standardization
  • AI standardizes what should be consistent across practice
22.2 Automation
  • Automate invisible workflow elements where possible
22.3 Human Preservation
  • Preserve and enhance what remains fundamentally human in care delivery
22.4 Continuous Learning
  • Learn from every case systematically (not just random cases)
  • Create feedback loops that improve system over time
22.5 Core Message
  • AI alone will not future-proof a practice
  • Learning systems with human-AI collaboration will succeed
