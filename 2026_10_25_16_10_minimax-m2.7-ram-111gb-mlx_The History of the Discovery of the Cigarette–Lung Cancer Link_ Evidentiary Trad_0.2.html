The History of the Discovery of the Cigarette–Lung Cancer Link: Evidentiary Traditions, Corporate Denial, Global Toll
  • Source: Tobacco Control 2012;21:87–91
  • Topic: History of cigarette-cancer link discovery, tobacco industry denial campaigns, and global mortality statistics
  • Author(s): Robert N Proctor
Lung Cancer: From Rarity to Epidemic
  • Lung cancer was once very rare --> doctors considered it a "once-in-a-lifetime" oddity
  • ~140 known cases in published medical literature as recently as 1900
  • Primary lung tumours found at autopsy became more common in late 19th century Germany
  • Isaac Adler published the world's first monograph on lung cancer in 1912
  • Noted "decided increase" in malignant neoplasms of the lung
  • Mentioned "abuse of tobacco and alcohol" as possible cause
  • Concluded the subject was "not yet ready for final judgment"
  • Mechanisation and mass marketing of cigarettes began in late 19th century
  • By 1920s, surgeons encountered lung cancer with increasing frequency
Early Theories on Cause (Pre-1940s)
  • Asphalt dust from newly tarred roads
  • Industrial air pollution
  • Latent effects of poison gas exposure (WWI)
  • Global influenza pandemic of 1918–1919
  • Tobacco abuse
Converging Lines of Evidence (1940s–1950s)
Population Studies
  • First case-control study: Franz Hermann Müller at Cologne Hospital, 1939
  • Compared 86 lung cancer cases to similar number of cancer-free controls
  • Showed people with lung cancer were far more likely to have smoked
  • Eberhard Schairer and Eric Schöniger at University of Jena, 1943
  • Even more ambitious study confirming Müller's findings
  • In 1950 alone, five separate epidemiological studies published:
  • Ernst Wynder and Evarts Graham (USA)
  • Richard Doll and A Bradford Hill (England)
  • All confirmed: cigarette smokers had far higher lung cancer rates than non-smokers
  • Prospective "cohort" studies conducted to eliminate recall bias:
  • Followed two separate healthy groups (smoking vs non-smoking) over time
  • Matched by age, sex, occupation and other traits
Key Cohort Study Findings
  • Doll and Hill 1954: Smokers of 35+ cigarettes/day had 40x increased odds of dying from lung cancer
  • Hammond and Horn 1954: Concluded link proven "beyond a reasonable doubt" (American Cancer Society study)
Animal Experimentation
  • "Tobacco juice" shown to cause cancer in lab animals since the first decade of the 20th century
  • Angel H Roffo (Argentina) --> founding director of Institute of Experimental Medicine for the Study and Treatment of Cancer
  • 1931: Showed smoke condensed from tobacco destructive distillation caused tumours on hairless rabbit skins
  • Published dozens of articles in 1930s and early 1940s (German and Spanish)
  • Ernst Wynder, Evarts Graham and Adele Croninger, 1953
  • Tumours generated by painting cigarette smoke tars onto shaved mouse backs
  • Life magazine devoted several pages to the story
  • Time cited Graham: case against tobacco proved "beyond any doubt"
  • Result: Public confidence in tobacco shaken, stock prices plummeted
  • German tobacco manufacturers established Chronica Nicotiana journal and Academia Nicotiana Internationalis to defend tobacco
Cellular Pathology
  • Pathologists in 1930s noticed cigarette smoke caused ciliastasis
  • Ciliastasis = deadening of tiny hair-like structures lining upper airway passages
  • These structures normally waft particulate contaminants out of the lungs
  • Suspicion grew that ciliastasis caused cigarette smoke to become trapped in lungs --> cancer
  • Anderson C Hilding 1956: Confirmed smokers experienced pulmonary ciliastasis
  • Cilia deadened at precisely those parts of the lung where cancers most likely develop
  • Oscar Auerbach (same era): Showed from autopsy studies that precancerous cellular changes detectable in smokers
  • Even in those who died from other causes
Cancer-Causing Chemicals in Cigarette Smoke
  • Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) identified as carcinogenic in coal tar in the 1930s
  • Question arose: might similar compounds exist in cigarette smoke?
  • Angel Roffo first to identify PAHs in cigarette smoke from distinctive spectrographic signatures
  • 1947: John B Fishel (Ohio State University) for Lorillard company
  • Internal report acknowledged "carcinogenic benzopyrene in tobacco tars"
  • Cited Roffo as authority
  • 1952: Brown and Williamson researchers identified benzpyrene in cigarette smoke
  • By end of 1950s, manufacturers had characterised several dozen carcinogens in cigarette smoke:
  • Arsenic
  • Chromium
  • Nickel
  • Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (chrysene, methylcholanthrene, dibenzanthracene, dibenzacridene, etc.)
  • Helmut Wakeham (Philip Morris research director), 1961
  • Noted carcinogens found in "practically every class of compounds in cigarette smoke"
Medical Authority Recognition
  • 1954: American Cancer Society National Board of Directors announced "without dissent" that evidence indicated "association between smoking, particularly cigarette smoking, and lung cancer"
  • 1954: Public Health Cancer Association advised stopping smoking to prevent cancer
  • Scandinavian countries (Norway, Sweden, Finland, Denmark) and The Netherlands reached similar conclusions
  • UK cancer authorities came on board
  • Joint Tuberculosis Society of Great Britain and Canada's National Department of Health and Welfare agreed
  • Medical attention shifted from "whether" to "how"
Tobacco Industry Response and Denial
The 1953 Conspiracy
  • December 14, 1953: CEOs of six largest tobacco manufacturers met at Plaza Hotel in Manhattan
  • All except Liggett participated
  • Planned far-reaching strategy to refute accumulating evidence
  • Used adverts, "white papers", press releases, corporate schmoozing with science writers and journalists
  • Called for "more research" to resolve a purported "controversy"
  • Per capita consumption rebounded after 1953 dip
  • Consumption continued to grow throughout 1960s and 1970s
  • Peaked at about 630 billion cigarettes in USA (1982) before declining
Internal Industry Knowledge
  • Tobacco industry insiders privately conceded cigarette-cancer link by mid-1950s
  • Never admitted publicly
Key Internal Documents
  • Claude Teague (RJ Reynolds), 1953 confidential "Survey of Cancer Research"
  • Concluded parallel rise in cigarette use and cancer led to suspicion tobacco was "an important etiologic factor in the induction of primary cancer of the lung"
  • Noted clinical data confirming relationship
  • Concluded large body of animal experimental work "would seem to indicate the presence of carcinogens"
  • Harris Parmele (Lorillard director of research), 1946
  • Commented: "Certain scientists and medical authorities have claimed for many years that the use of tobacco contributes to cancer development in susceptible people. Just enough evidence has been presented to justify the possibility of such a presumption"
  • 1953: American Tobacco Company secretly sponsored animal tests at Ecusta Paper Corporation laboratories
  • Goal: determine if tobacco leaf or cigarette paper caused cancer
  • Conclusion (distributed only privately): tobacco was the culprit
Internal Acknowledgements in 1950s
  • Tobacco company research director (December 1953): "Boy! Wouldn't it be wonderful if our company was first to produce a cancer-free cigarette. What we could do to competition!"
  • Another remarked on smoker addiction: "a habit they can't break"
  • 1958: Three leading scientists from British American Tobacco (BAT) visited USA
  • Found that with only one exception, all consulted experts believed cancer connection had been proved
  • Alan Rodgman (Reynolds), 1962: Confessed evidence for cancer link was "overwhelming"; evidence against was "scant"
  • Helmut Wakeham (Philip Morris): Drew up list of dozens of carcinogens in cigarette smoke
The 1964 Surgeon General's Report
  • Often regarded as turning point in recognition of health harms from smoking
  • Actually a "scientific anticlimax": case against smoking was closed by end of 1950s
  • Only the "truculence and obstinacy" of cigarette manufacturers forced federal review
  • Charles S Cameron (American Cancer Society), 1956 Atlantic Monthly:
  • "If the same level of evidence had been arrayed against, say, spinach, no one would have objected to the banning of that plant from the national diet"
Popular Knowledge and Ignorance
Public Opinion Polling
  • 1954 (Gallup): Asked if cigarette smoking is "one of the causes of lung cancer"
  • Only 41% answered "yes"
  • Remainder answered either "no" or "undecided"
  • 1960: American Cancer Society poll
  • Only one-third of all US doctors agreed cigarette smoking should be considered "a major cause of lung cancer"
  • 43% of all American doctors were still smoking cigarettes regularly
  • Another 5% were occasional users
Industry Campaigns to Manufacture Doubt
  • Cigarette makers spent vast sums to deny and distract from cigarette-cancer link
  • Industry quantified impact of denialist propaganda
Example: 1972 Propaganda Film "Smoking and Health: The Need to Know"
  • Shown to hundreds of thousands, including high school students
  • Audience testing by Tobacco Institute (AHF-Basico Market Research Co. and Audience Studies, Inc.)
  • Results: Film reduced by 17.8% the number agreeing "Cigarette smoking cause[s] lung cancer"
  • From 74.9% to 57.1%
  • Film also produced "significant shifts" in attitudes favourable to industry:
  • ↑ number agreeing recent reports had "overemphasized the dangers of smoking"
Global Denial Campaigns
  • 1980s: UK tobacco researchers noted Philip Morris was piloting a "global strategy" to deny secondhand smoke hazards
  • Spending vast sums "to keep the controversy alive"
  • 2011: International Tobacco Control Policy Evaluation Project (Netherlands)
  • Survey showed only 61% of Dutch adults agreed cigarette smoke endangered non-smokers
  • Hundreds of millions of Chinese remain poorly informed about smoking hazards
The Global Toll
Scale of the Epidemic
  • Cigarette is "the deadliest artefact in the history of human civilization"
  • ~1.5 million deaths from lung cancer per year globally (extrapolated from 2008 IARC data)
  • ~95% of lung cancer deaths are entirely preventable
  • Number expected to rise to nearly 2 million per year by 2020s or 2030s
  • Even if consumption rates decline, deaths will continue for decades
Mortality Statistics
  • 1 death per 3–4 million cigarettes smoked
  • Latency period: ~25 years
  • 6 trillion cigarettes smoked annually globally
  • One death every 5 seconds from smoking-related causes
  • One-third to one-quarter of deaths are lung cancer (one every 15–20 seconds)
  • Only about 100 million people died from smoking in the 20th century
  • Several times that likely to die in present century even if rates decline dramatically
China's Role
  • Chinese cigarette consumption rose from ~500 billion (1980) to over 2 trillion (2010)
  • China now manufactures about 2.4 trillion cigarettes per year
  • ~40% of global total
  • Unclear whether consumption has peaked
  • Global cigarette use likely peaked at ~6 trillion after 2000
Manufacturing Scale and Speed
  • Modern cigarette making machines (Hauni Corporation Hamburg, GD Bologna) crank out up to 20,000 cigarettes per minute
  • Hongta's Yuxi Cigarette Factory produces over 90 billion cigarettes per year using 52 high-speed Molins machines
  • ~400 industrial-scale cigarette factories in the world

Value of Human Life to Cigarette Manufacturers
  • Cigarette companies make about 1 cent profit per cigarette sold
  • ~$10,000 profit for every million cigarettes purchased
  • One death occurs per million cigarettes sold/smoked
  • Value of a human life to a cigarette manufacturer: ~$10,000
  • A factory such as Hongta's in Yuxi responsible for 25,000–30,000 lung cancer deaths per year
  • About twice that number from other diseases
Physical Scale Calculations
  • 6 trillion cigarettes smoked annually
  • Each cigarette ~60mm (counting only the part smoked)
  • 360 trillion mm of cigarettes smoked per year
  • = 360 billion metres or 360 million km
  • Rate: more than 10 km per second
  • Equivalent to orbital speed of satellites around Earth
Weight Calculations
  • Cigarettes contain about two-thirds of a gram of tobacco
  • ~2 million grams (2 metric tonnes) of tobacco to cause one lung cancer
  • Typical tobacco farm yields ~2 tonnes per hectare
  • A 10-hectare field causes approximately:
  • 10 lung cancer deaths per year
  • ~20 additional deaths from heart attacks, gangrene of feet, bladder cancers, oral cavity cancers
Broader Causation Networks
  • Cigarette epidemic facilitated by long causal chains:
  • Sociopolitical
  • Technical
  • Molecular
  • Agricultural
  • Advertisements can be carcinogens
  • Convenience stores and pharmacies that sell cigarettes are part of the causation network
  • Those who design cigarette packs cause cancer
  • PR and advertising firms managing tobacco accounts cause cancer
  • Farmers who grow tobacco are part of the network
  • Politicians who take money from "Big Tobacco" cause cancer
  • Chemists and breeders who favour the nicotine molecule cause cancer
  • Experts who testify for industry in court cause harm
Key Myths Addressed
  • Myth: Dangers of smoking have long been "common knowledge"
  • Reality: Public was slow to accept; denial campaigns actively manufactured doubt
  • Myth: Legitimate scholarly doubt about hazards postdates 1964 Surgeon General's report
  • Reality: Case was closed by end of 1950s; doubt was manufactured
  • Myth: Tobacco "problem" has been solved (like dietary problems)
  • Reality: ~6 trillion cigarettes smoked annually; most of epidemic still in the future
