1. Transnasal Endoscopic Ultrasound for Esophageal Wall Motion Assessment
1.1 Study Overview
  • Objective: Establish quantitative assessment of esophageal wall motion using transnasal endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)
  • Cohort: 9 healthy controls + 57 achalasia patients
  • Achalasia subtypes (Chicago Classification v3.0):
  • Type 1: 37 patients
  • Type 2: 11 patients
  • Type 3: 9 patients
1.2 Technical Setup
  • Transnasal endoscope + 20 MHz miniature US probe (2.2 mm diameter)
  • Patient remains fully awake, non-sedated
  • Probes fixed at 3 sites: LES + 5 cm + 10 cm proximally
  • 20 mL oral rehydration solution swallowed for assessment
  • Videos converted to 30 frames at 0.1 second intervals
1.3 Quantitative Parameters Defined
  • Muscular Contraction Ratio = muscle layer thickness at contraction phase ÷ resting phase
  • Esophageal Wall Distensibility Ratio = luminal area at distension ÷ resting phase
1.4 Four Phases of Swallowing
  • Resting (pre-distension)
  • Distension (maximal area reached)
  • Contraction (minimal areas)
  • Relaxation
1.5 Results
  • Safety: >15 cases with zero procedure-related complications
  • Reliability: High correlation for same observers + different observers
  • Type 3 achalasia → significantly higher muscular contraction ratio vs Type 1 in lower esophagus
  • No significant difference in middle esophagus among subtypes
  • Type 3 achalasia → significantly higher distensibility ratio vs other subtypes in both distal and middle esophagus
1.6 Clinical Observations
  • Type 2: Impaired distensibility + complete loss of contraction
  • Type 3: Both contractility and distensibility preserved
2. 4D HRM Metrics for EGJ Outflow Obstruction Evaluation
2.1 Study Design
  • Retrospective observational cohort (Northwestern Medicine Motility Registry 2014–2024)
  • n = 123 patients with EGJOO on HRM + completed FLIP + TBE
  • Mean age: 59 years, 60% female
  • Primary indication: dysphagia in 89% of patients
2.2 4D HRM Derivation Process
  • Six swallow landmarks manually identified per study
  • Four phases delineated:
  • Accommodation (esophagus relaxes for bolus)
  • Compartmentalization (bolus contained in esophageal body)
  • Peristalsis + esophageal emptying
  • Ampulla emptying (complete distal clearance)
2.3 Three Key 4D Metrics
  • 4D EGJ DI = maximum EGJ cross-sectional area ÷ intraluminal pressure during phase 3
  • 4D Max EGJ Diameter = maximum EGJ diameter during phase 3
  • 4D Clearance Ratio = 1 – (post-peristalsis bolus volume ÷ pre-peristalsis volume)
  • Closer to 0 = impaired emptying
  • Closer to 1 = effective emptying
2.4 FLIP Classification (Dallas Consensus v2.0)
  • Normal EGJ opening
  • Reduced EGJ opening
  • Borderline EGJ opening
2.5 TBE Classification
  • TBE positive
  • TBE negative
  • TBE intermediate
2.6 Key Findings
  • 4D EGJ DI and 4D Max Diameter → significant differences across FLIP classes
  • Normal/borderline groups > reduced group (both metrics)
  • 4D Clearance Ratio → aligned with TBE retention classes
  • Combined outcome: all 3 metrics differed significantly across groups
2.7 Clinical Decision Points
  • 0 patients with 4D EGJ diameter <8 mm → negative flip/TBE outcome
  • 0 patients with 4D EGJ DI >2.1 AND diameter >10.1 → positive flip/TBE outcome
  • Intermediate class: abnormal EGJ metrics + 8/14 had abnormal clearance ratio
3. Histopathology-Based Subtyping of Achalasia
3.1 Study Population
  • n = 100 patients with achalasia undergoing POEM
  • LES samples collected → paraffin-embedded specimens
3.2 Pathological Parameters Assessed
  • Neural: total neurons, nNOS neurons, glial cells
  • Immune: total immune cells, B cells, T cells, macrophages, mast cells, eosinophils
  • Fibrosis: Masson staining grade
3.3 Thrombotic Burden Findings
  • Achalasia patients → significantly higher thrombotic burden vs controls (n=19 additional)
  • Complement/coagulation cascade proteins upregulated in pre-omic studies
3.4 Hierarchical Clustering Results → Four Subtypes
3.4.1 Subtype 1 (n=59) – Neuron Loss Type
  • Prominent reduction in total + nNOS neurons
  • Higher IRP
  • Classic neuron loss pattern
3.4.2 Subtype 2 (n=8) – Neural Preserve/Low Injury
  • Significantly higher total neuron counts
  • Lower IRP
  • Neuron preservation despite similar immune infiltration to Subtype 1
3.4.3 Subtype 3 (n=4) – Immune-Mediated Type
  • Higher immune cells: T cells, B cells, macrophages, nNOS neurons
  • Resembles "immune height + neuron preserve" phenotype (Group A from prior studies)
  • May represent slowly progressive pattern
3.4.4 Subtype 4 (n=29) – Coagulation/Vascular-Related
  • Significantly higher thrombus burden + IRP
  • Lower total and nNOS neuron counts
  • Points to coagulation/vascular pathway of neural injury
3.5 Key Observations
  • Subtypes 1 & 2 show negative correlation between IRP and neuron counts
  • Similar immune infiltration despite different neuron preservation → non-immune-mediated pathogenic mechanisms exist
3.6 No Significant Differences Among Subtypes
  • Age, gender, BMI, disease duration, Eckardt score
  • Chicago classification distribution
  • Other manometric parameters
  • Myotomy length or recurrence rates
4. AI Transformer Model for FLIP Manometry Classification
4.1 Background
  • FLIP manometry v2.0 (Dallas consensus) used for classification
  • Prior CNN-based models relied on version 1.0
  • Inter-rater reliability among specialists for FLIP: moderate to good only
4.2 Transformer Architecture Advantages
  • Self-attention mechanism → focuses on relevant input data
  • Captures spatial AND temporal dependencies (vs CNNs' local feature extraction)
  • Well-suited for longitudinal physiologic data
4.3 Study Cohort
  • n = 984 patients (2013–2024)
  • Mean age: 53 years, 59% female
  • Distribution:
  • Normal manometry: 36%
  • Abnormal manometry: 25%
  • Achalasia: 39%
4.4 Cost-Sensitive Penalty Matrix
  • 0 cost: correct classifications
  • 100 cost: critical misclassifications (e.g., achalasia → normal)
  • 10–50 cost: diagnostically adjacent errors (e.g., spasm → obstruction)
4.5 Model Performance
  • Overall accuracy: 89%
  • Normal: sensitivity 89%, specificity 97%
  • Abnormal: sensitivity 85%, specificity 91%
  • Achalasia: sensitivity 93%, specificity 96%
  • No achalasia classified as normal
  • No normal classified as achalasia
4.6 Cost Matrix Impact
  • Eliminated all high-priority misclassifications
  • Reduced moderate misclassifications
  • Remaining errors: cost penalty ≤25
4.7 Comparison to Prior CNN Models
  • Binary accuracy improved from prior models → 94%
  • Three-class accuracy: 89%
4.8 Clinical Concordance
  • Normal FLIP → 99% NPV for achalasia/conclusive EGJOO
  • Non-spastic obstruction on FLIP → 91% PPV for achalasia/conclusive EGJOO
  • AI model performance matches human expert concordance rates
5. IRP Rapid Drink Challenge Predicts Pneumatic Dilation Outcomes
5.1 Study Design
  • Single-center cohort (2020–2025)
  • n = 52 patients underwent PD with 30 mm balloon
  • 46 patients completed 5-year follow-up
  • Post-treatment HRM at 1 month + TBE
5.2 Follow-up Protocol
  • Eckardt score every 3 months
  • TBE annually or when Eckardt ≥3
  • Reintervention: Eckardt ≥3 + TBE retention
5.3 Outcomes
  • 59% achieved remission at 5 years
  • 41% required reintervention (graded PD or POEM)
5.4 Post-Treatment Findings
  • 56% in remission group had ATA features → significantly higher than 16% in reintervention group
  • Median IRP (both positions) → significantly lower in remission vs reintervention group
  • Peristaltic reappearance: higher in remission (not significant)
5.5 Rapid Drink Challenge Results
  • IRP RDC significantly lower in remission vs reintervention patients
  • No difference in PEP during RDC between groups
5.6 Cox Proportional Hazard Analysis
  • Predictors of reintervention at 5 years:
  • TAOO presence
  • High IRP RDC
  • High median IRP upright
  • Multivariate: only high IRP RDC remained significant
5.7 ROC Analysis for IRP RDC
  • AUC = 0.87
  • Cutoff ≥11 mmHg → predicts reintervention
  • Hazard ratio: 12.6
  • Accuracy: ~81%
5.8 Survival Analysis
  • IRP RDC <11: 87% reintervention-free at 5 years
  • IRP RDC ≥11: median time to reintervention = 9 months
  • ATA patients: 83% reintervention-free at 5 years
  • TAOO patients: median time to reintervention = 13 months
6. External Abdominal Compression for Contractile Reserve
6.1 Study Population
  • n = 23 healthy participants
  • Younger group (<70): n=15
  • Older group (>70): n=8
6.2 Protocol
  • Supine position throughout
  • Single swallows: 5 mL × 6 at 30-second intervals
  • MRS sequences: 5 × 2 mL swallows at 2–3 second intervals
  • MRS performed 3 times
  • All swallows with AND without 150 mmHg abdominal compression
6.3 Device
  • Non-stretchable belt + blood pressure cuff
  • Pressure gauge on upper abdomen
6.4 Contractile Reserve Definition
  • Reserve = Mean MRS DCI ÷ Single swallow DCI
  • Value >1 indicates presence of reserve
6.5 Key Findings – Single Swallow
  • Abdominal compression → significantly higher DCI in both age groups
6.6 Key Findings – MRS
  • Participants <70: compression → significantly higher DCI
  • Participants >70: effect did NOT reach statistical significance
6.7 Age Group Comparisons
  • MRS DCI (with/without compression) → significantly lower in older vs younger
  • Single swallow DCI: numerically lower in older but not significant
6.8 Contractile Reserve Spectrum (Younger Group)
  • Reserve 1 (MRS alone): 9/15 had positive reserve
  • Reserve 2 (+ compression): 3 additional participants met criteria → total 12/15
  • Reserve 3 (above MRS alone): 14/15 could generate contraction beyond MRS
6.9 Older Group Findings
  • 5/8 participants: MRS DCI with compression < single swallow DCI
  • No additional participants qualified for positive MRS test with compression
6.10 Clinical Implications
  • External abdominal compression is a simple adjunct to standard provocative maneuvers
  • May improve assessment of esophageal motor function
  • Future applications: patients with diseased esophagus/esophageal motility disorders
7. Neuromodulator Therapy in Non-Obstructive Dysphagia
7.1 Study Framework
  • Gut-brain axis model: symptoms may reflect hypersensitivity, central processing, anxiety, hypervigilance
  • Symptom severity often disproportionate to measurable pathology
7.2 Esophageal Hypervigilance and Anxiety Scale (EHAS)
  • Validated patient-reported outcome
  • Originally 15 questions → shortened to 7 items
  • Two subscores: anxiety + hypervigilance
7.3 Study Design
  • Single-center longitudinal exploratory cohort (Brigham & Women's Hospital)
  • Adults undergoing esophageal function testing
  • All off PPI therapy for 24-hour pH impedance + HRM
  • Excluded: prior foregut surgery, achalasia spectrum disorders
7.4 Patient-Reported Outcomes
  • EHAS (7-item short form)
  • BEDQ (Brief Esophageal Dysphagia Questionnaire)
  • GSS (Global Symptom Severity)
7.5 Cohort Characteristics
  • n = 31 patients with complete data
  • Mean age: 59 years, predominantly female
  • Average follow-up: ~475 days
  • Known anxiety diagnosis: 26%
  • Known depression diagnosis: 34%
  • Prior neuromodulator use: only 6.5%
7.6 Baseline Physiology
  • Relatively preserved motility and pH-impedance findings overall
  • Only 16% had hiatal hernia
7.7 Baseline Symptom Burden Predictors
  • EHAS total and EHAS anxiety → strongest correlation with BEDQ/GSS
  • Hypervigilance subscore: positive but weaker correlation
  • Standard motility parameters → weak, non-significant relationships
7.8 Treatment Response
  • BEDQ: mean improvement of 34%
  • GSS: mean improvement of nearly 29% (statistically significant)
  • Major clinical response (>50% improvement):
  • BEDQ: 48% of patients
  • GSS: 42% of patients
7.9 Predictors of Response
  • Reduction in EHAS total and EHAS anxiety → significantly associated with symptom improvement
  • EHAS hypervigilance alone → NOT significantly correlated
7.10 Responders vs Non-Responders
  • Responders (≥50% improvement) showed greater EHAS reduction
  • Anxiety subscore showed clearest separation between groups
7.11 Multivariable Regression (Adjusted for)
  • Age, sex, BMI
  • Percent normal swallows
  • Bolus clearance
7.12 Independent Predictors of Improvement
  • Change in EHAS total → significant predictor for BEDQ AND GSS
  • Change in EHAS anxiety → significant predictor for BEDQ AND GSS
7.13 Neuromodulator Distribution
  • >80% started on TCAs
  • Gabapentin second most common
7.14 Proposed Mechanism Model
  • Symptom amplification cycle:
  • Symptoms → anxiety → heightened perception → worsened symptoms
  • Interventions targeting gut-brain axis may break this cycle
7.15 Clinical Recommendations
  • EHAS may serve as practical tool for patient selection
  • Non-obstructive dysphagia → approach as physiologic + gut-brain interaction disorder
  • Potential therapies: neuromodulators, brain-gut behavioral therapy (CBT)
8. Key Methodological Considerations Across Studies
8.1 Diagnostic Limitations Acknowledged
  • HRM cannot directly assess esophageal distensibility or muscular contractions
  • ~40% of dysphagia patients have normal HRM findings
  • Standard testing may miss: longitudinal muscle function, distension abnormalities
8.2 FLIP/HRM/TBE Integration
  • Current guidelines require additional confirmatory testing for EGJOO before treatment
  • 4D HRM metrics may reduce need for multiple procedures
8.3 AI Integration Potential
  • FLIP data alone contains sufficient information for manometry-level predictions
  • Cost-sensitive training reduces clinically consequential errors
8.4 Treatment Response Prediction
  • Post-treatment physiologic parameters (IRP RDC) may predict long-term outcomes
  • Enables individualized follow-up strategies
