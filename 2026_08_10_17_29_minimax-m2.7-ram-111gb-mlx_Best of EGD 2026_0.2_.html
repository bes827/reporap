Barrett's Esophagus: Indefinite for Dysplasia (BE-IND)
  • BE-IND encountered in up to 8.4% of patients
  • Management challenge due to ↑ propensity for progression
White et al 23-Year Retrospective Study (223 patients)
  • Surveillance EGD within 1 year after diagnosis
  • ~10% developed prevalent neoplasia:
  • Low grade dysplasia (LGD): 3.1%
  • High grade dysplasia (HGD): 5.4%
  • Early adenocarcinoma: 1.4%
  • 22% of patients without neoplasia → incident neoplasia
  • Median follow-up: 4.37 years
Risk Factors for Progression
  • BE length: 13% more likely to develop dysplasia --> each extra centimeter
  • Smoking: >2x risk for progression
Clinical Implications
  • ↑ surveillance warranted in BE-IND population
  • Adjunctive tests may help phenotype differences:
  • WATS-3D
  • p53 assessment
  • TissueCypher
Editorial Recommendation
  • Dr. Muthusamy's editorial --> essential reading for esophageal endoscopists
Gastric Intestinal Metaplasia: Indefinite for Dysplasia (GIM-IND)
Global Burden of Gastric Cancer (GC)
  • 5th leading cause of cancer-related death globally
  • 2–13x greater incidence in non-White individuals
  • Higher rates --> early-generation immigrants
  • US 5-year survival: 36% (↓ compared to global benchmarks)
Western Practice Limitations
  • ↓ confidence in identification, surveillance, management vs Eastern colleagues
  • ACG guidelines first published: 2019 (AGA) and 2025 (ACG)
  • GIM-IND widely disregarded due to:
  • Interobserver variability
  • Downstaging to "no dysplasia"
  • Uncommon nomenclature by Western pathologists
ACG 2025 Guideline Statement on GIM-IND
  • "May arguably represent inflammatory/regenerative process vs true neoplastic transformation"
  • Recommended management:
  • Mucosal resection when margins visible (IND, LGD, HGD)
  • OR 6–12 month second-look endoscopy
Moon et al Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis
  • 16 studies pooled
  • 2,226 lesions with forceps-obtained, criteria-satisfying GIM-IND
  • Comparison: immediate endoscopic resection vs conservative rebiopsy
Outcomes Comparison
Group GC Rate
Immediate resection 66%
Rebiopsy group 20%
  • Management decisions driven by histology + endoscopic + patient factors
Clinical Implications (Shiratori Editorial)
  • "Quick" Western EGD may underestimate malignant potential
  • Inadequate mucosal assessment for subtle early GC indicators
  • Sampling error with standard forceps biopsy
  • Study demonstrates limitations of current management strategies
Cryoablation for Barrett's Esophagus: Coldplay 3 Trial
Historical Context
  • Balloon cryoablation (BCA) --> historically salvage therapy
  • Limited data on long-term durability and recurrence management
Coldplay 3 Trial (Canto et al)
  • Prospective, 11-center trial
  • 76 patients with BE (up to 6 cm length)
  • Included: LGD, HGD, intramucosal adenocarcinoma
  • Previously achieved complete eradication with BCA
Outcomes
  • BE recurrence: ~12%
  • Median time to recurrence: 19 months
  • With retreatment in recurrent cases:
  • Complete eradication maintained: 98.6%
  • Only 1 patient had progression to adenocarcinoma
European Multicenter Trial (Frederiks et al)
  • 107 patients with dysplasia or early cancer
  • BE limits: circumferential ≤2 cm, marginal ≤5 cm
  • Entry characteristics:
  • Median short segment BE
  • 65% prior endoscopic resection
  • 40% add-on treatment
Outcomes
  • Complete eradication of visible BE: 100%
  • Histologically: 96%
  • Median 18-month follow-up:
  • 96% free of endoscopically visible BE
  • Strictures: 13%
Comparison to Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)
  • Sachdeva et al comparative study
  • Comparable BE and dysplasia recurrence rates
Clinical Implications
  • BCA now viable primary strategy for BE spectrum
  • Durability demonstrated --> re-induction of complete eradication possible
Eosinophilic Esophagitis: ASGE Guidelines Part 2
ASGE Consensus Recommendations (2026)
  • Focus on endoscopic considerations
  • Applicable to pediatric and adult patients
Pediatric Considerations
  • Self-reporting difficulties --> surrogate indicators paramount
  • Clinical triggers for endoscopic evaluation:
  • Malnutrition correlates
  • Feeding difficulties
  • Vomiting
  • Treatment-refractory reflux
  • Failure to thrive
Adult Considerations
  • Dysphagia --> most common presenting symptom in adults
  • Pediatric patients unable to verbalize/contextualize dysphagia
Endoscopic Imaging Adjuncts
  • Fluoroscopic esophageal imaging (esophagram) --> supplement endoscopy
  • Both pediatric and adult patients
  • EndoFLIP role --> not yet determined
Diagnostic Limitations Revealed
Pediatric Population
  • 55% of pediatric EOE patients had strictures on esophagram
  • Strictures missed by endoscopy alone
Adult Population
  • Endoscopy sensitivity for strictures: 25%
  • Esophageal diameters <15 mm often missed on endoscopy
  • Confirmed by EndoFlip measurements
Post-Bariatric Surgery: Endoscopic Management
US Bariatric Surgery Volume (2023)
  • Sleeve gastrectomy (SG): >157,000 procedures
  • Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB): >63,000 procedures

Sleeve Gastrectomy: Weight Regain
  • 75% of patients --> weight regain within 6 years
  • Surgical revision rate: 20% at 7 years post-SG
Jirapinyo et al Retrospective Cohort Study (162 patients)
  • Propensity-matched comparison
  • Endoscopic suturing vs surgical re-sleeve for post-SG weight regain

RYGB: Weight Regain and Dumping Syndrome
Weight Regain Statistics
  • Patients regain 30% of lost weight within 10 years
  • One-third --> majority of lost weight regained
Gastrojejunal Anastomosis (GJA) Role
  • ↑ diameter --> major factor for recurrent weight gain
  • Historically managed with surgical revision
Transoral Outlet Reduction (TORe)
  • Endoscopic suturing approach
  • Reduces diameter of gastroenterostomy stoma
Outcomes (5-year durability demonstrated)
  • Technical success: 100%
  • TBWL at 1 year: 9–17%
  • Safety profile superior to surgical revision
McCarty et al Standardization Consensus
  • Consortium of TORe experts (>300 procedures each)
  • Published Delphi method consensus document
Standardization Elements
  • Cognitive skills requirements
  • Technical considerations
  • Suture pattern protocols
  • Starting location for suturing
  • Final cinch location guidelines
  • Post-completion examination standards
Dumping Syndrome (DS) After RYGB
  • Occurs in roughly 13% of patients
  • Refractory to conservative approaches (dietary + pharmacologic)
Hayat et al Meta-Analysis
  • 4 studies, 241 patients
  • All underwent TORe for DS
Outcomes
Metric Value
Technical success 100%
Sigstad Score improvement 13.7 points (strong clinical success)
Adverse event rate ~10%
Re-intervention rate 8%
Topical Hemostatic Therapies: Acute NV-GIH
Agents Available
  • Hemostatic powders
  • Self-assembling peptide gels
Laine Invited Review (2026)
  • Assesses efficacy of topical agents
  • Primary endpoints: primary hemostasis, persistent/recurrent hemorrhage
Methodology Strengths
  • Only RCTs or meta-analyses of RCTs included
  • High-quality evidence synthesis
Clinical Utility
  • Paper summarizes high-quality data for each option
  • Details specific circumstances validated in RCTs
  • Critiques study limitations
Recommendation for Clinicians
  • Excellent resource for brushing up on sprays and gels
  • Applicable to non-variceal upper GI bleeding scenarios
