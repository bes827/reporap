Benign Recurrent Esophageal Strictures: Paclitaxel-Coated Balloons to the Rescue?
  • Source: Gastrointestinal Endoscopy, Vol. ■, No. ■ : 2026
  • Topic: Treatment of recurrent benign esophageal strictures using drug-coated balloons (DCB)
  • Author(s): Brandon Rodgers, MD (Penn State Milton S. Hershey Medical Center)
1. Definition of Recurrent Esophageal Strictures
  • Inability to maintain satisfactory luminal diameter for 4 weeks after reaching target diameter of 14 mm via dilation
  • Significant impact on patient quality of life
2. Etiology of Benign Refractory Strictures
  • Surgical intervention
  • Radiation therapy
  • Caustic ingestion
  • Often challenging to treat because of their fibrotic nature
3. Standard Treatment Approaches
  • Serial dilations with balloon or bougie dilator
  • Majority of patients show improvement in dysphagia after standard dilation therapy
3.1. Recurrence Statistics
  • 30%–40% of patients develop recurrent dysphagia after first dilation
  • Up to 10% of patients develop refractory strictures despite multiple dilations
4. Alternative Treatment Modalities
  • Dilation combined with triamcinolone injection therapy
  • Dilation combined with mitomycin C injection therapy
  • Incisional therapy
  • Stent placement
  • Each approach has notable limitations
5. Wang et al Study Overview
5.1. Study Design
  • Prospective substudy of upcoming randomized controlled clinical trial
  • First published U.S. experience using paclitaxel DCB for recurrent esophageal strictures
5.2. Patient Enrollment Criteria
  • Minimum 2 prior dilations
  • Ongoing solid food dysphagia
  • Stricture diameter <13 mm
  • Stricture length ≤5 cm
5.3. Enrollment Numbers
  • 25 patients enrolled from 10 centers
6. Study Protocol Details
6.1. Procedure Steps
  • Strictures predilated with standard balloon to 1–2 mm less than target diameter
  • Target diameter initially ≥15 mm, later increased to ≥18 mm
  • Followed by dilation using DCB to achieve target diameter
6.2. Measurement Methodology
  • Stricture diameter measured using endoluminal functional lumen imaging probe (EndoFLIP)
  • EndoFLIP by Medtronic, Minneapolis, Minn, USA
  • More objective than endoscopist estimation (reduces intraobserver and interobserver variability)
7. Study Results
7.1. Dilation Success Rate
  • 76% of patients achieved dilation size of at least 18 mm
7.2. Freedom from Stricture Recurrence
  • 40% at 12 months
7.3. Annualized Intervention Rate
  • Improved from 7.5 to 1.2 interventions per year
  • Comparison: patient's own historical dilation requirements
  • Statistical significance: P < .001
7.4. Pharmacokinetic and Safety Results
  • 28% of patients had measurable levels of paclitaxel
  • 4 patients experienced adverse events (all classified as mild)
8. Comparative Effectiveness
8.1. Versus Esophageal Stent Placement
  • Similar patency rates at 12 months
  • DCB dilation unlikely to require additional endoscopic training
  • No risk of stent migration or other stent-related adverse events
8.2. Versus Alternative Therapies (Balloon + Triamcinolone vs Incisional Therapy + Steroid)
  • Median restructure-free survival: 10.6 weeks (balloon) vs 8.7 weeks (incisional)
  • Number of endoscopic dilations in 24-week follow-up: 1 (balloon) vs 2 (incisional)
  • DCB results appear promising compared with these modalities
9. Study Strengths
9.1. Methodology Advantages
  • First published U.S. experience with paclitaxel DCB for this indication
  • Standardized endoscopic protocol
  • Use of validated outcomes
  • EndoFLIP measurement for objective primary outcome assessment
9.2. Clinical Significance
  • 40% freedom from recurrence at 12 months is significant and encouraging
  • Reduction in annualized intervention rate from 7.5 to 1.2 represents an 83% improvement
10. Study Limitations
10.1. Design Limitations
  • Nonrandomized design
  • Use of historical dilation requirements as main comparator
  • Prone to influence from multiple confounding factors
10.2. Confounding Variables
  • Endoscopist performing dilations affects outcomes
  • Outcomes likely superior when performed by expert endoscopists at tertiary referral centers
  • Standardized dilation protocol may have partially mitigated this effect
10.3. Sample Size Limitations
  • Small number of patients (n = 25)
  • Larger studies required to further validate findings
10.4. Interpretation Caveats
  • Positive results may not be attributable to DCB alone
  • Results could reflect dilation protocol and/or procedural expertise
11. Ongoing and Future Research
11.1. PATENT-E Trial
  • Prospective randomized controlled clinical trial currently underway
  • Full name: Paclitaxel Coated Balloon for the Treatment of Chronic bEnigN sTricture-Esophagus
  • Expected to address limitations of current study
11.2. Future Research Directions
  • Role of repeated dilations with DCB
  • Use of DCB upfront as part of initial dilation strategy
  • Combination therapy with other stricture treatment modalities
11.3. Safety Monitoring Requirements
  • Chemotherapeutic agents involved (paclitaxel)
  • Need for ongoing safety monitoring
  • Ensuring limited systemic exposure is necessary
12. Conclusions and Clinical Implications
12.1. Key Findings Summary
  • Initial U.S. experience with paclitaxel-coated dilation balloon shows promising results
  • Sample size is small but findings are encouraging
  • 83% reduction in annualized intervention rate observed
12.2. Clinical Relevance
  • DCB represents a potentially valuable addition to treatment options for this challenging condition
  • May provide durable response where standard dilation fails
12.3. Path Forward
  • Findings will need validation in larger randomized controlled trial
  • DCB may become part of the treatment armamentarium for benign refractory esophageal strictures
