Esophageal Motility Disorders: Pathogenesis, Diagnosis, and AI Applications
  • Source: DDW (Digestive Disease Week) - Softgel Motility Disorders Session
  • Topic: Esophageal motility disorders, gut-brain interaction, and AI applications
  • Author(s): Narayaki Manabe, Adam Smires, Dani Shieh, Ofer Fass, Panchara Samvarnarapanesh, Khan, Andrew Jenkins, et al.
1. Esophageal Wall Motion Assessment Methods
1.1 Transnasal Endoscopic Ultrasound (TNE-US) - Manabe
  • Methodology
  • Combines transnasal endoscopy + 20 MHz miniature EUS probe (2.2 mm diameter)
  • Patient remains fully awake, non-sedated
  • Probe fixed at 3 sites: ~5 cm above LES, LES level, ~10 cm above LES
  • Patient swallows 20 mL bolus of oral dehydration solution
  • Recorded video converted to 3 frames at 0.1 second intervals
  • Measures esophagogastric luminal area + muscular layer thickness
  • Quantitative Parameters Defined
  • Muscular contraction ratio = muscle layer thickness after contraction phase ÷ resting phase
  • Esophageal wall distensibility ratio = luminal area at distension phase ÷ resting phase
  • Four Distinct Phases Identified
  • Resting phase (pre-distension)
  • Distension phase (maximal area reached)
  • Contraction phase (minimal luminal area)
  • Relaxation phase
  • Safety & Validation
  • >15 cases with zero procedure-related complications
  • High correlation between different observers (strong reliability)
  • Excellent reproducibility demonstrated
1.2 4D High-Resolution Manometry (HRM) - Smires
  • Background
  • HRM is standard for Chicago Classification v4.0 esophageal motility diagnosis
  • EGJOO diagnosed on HRM alone is always clinically inconclusive
  • Chicago Classification requires additional confirmatory testing (FLIP, TBE)
  • 4D HRM Concept
  • Builds on standard HRM (time, space, pressure domains)
  • Adds 4th metric: cross-sectional area derived from impedance
  • No additional procedures required (uses data already collected)
  • Derivation process:
  • Manually identify 6 swallow landmarks per study
  • Software delineates 4 phases: accommodation, compartmentalization, peristalsis/emptying, ampullary emptying
  • Impedance-derived estimates of intraluminal CSA and diameter
  • Three Key 4D Metrics
  • 4D EGJ Distensibility Index = max EGJ CSA ÷ intraluminal pressure during phase 3
  • 4D Max EGJ Diameter = maximum EGJ diameter achieved during phase 3
  • 4D Clearance Ratio = 1 minus (post-peristalsis bolus volume ÷ pre-peristalsis volume)
  • Value closer to 0 = impaired emptying
  • Value closer to 1 = effective emptying
  • Study Design
  • Retrospective observational cohort (Northwestern Medicine Motility Registry, 2014-2024)
  • Included: HRM-classified EGJOO + completed FLIP + TBE
  • Excluded: prior foregut surgery, pneumatic dilation, mechanical obstruction
2. Results: Esophageal Wall Assessment Studies
2.1 TNE-US Achalasia Subtype Analysis (n=57)
  • Demographics by Chicago Classification Type
  • Type 1: n=37
  • Type 2: n=11
  • Type 3: n=9
  • Type 3 patients tend to be older than other subtypes
  • No significant differences in clinical characteristics among subtypes
  • Muscular Contraction Ratio Findings
  • Type 3 achalasia shows significantly higher ratio vs Type 1 in distal sweeps
  • No significant differences among subtypes in middle sweeps
  • Esophageal Wall Distensibility Ratio Findings
  • Type 3 achalasia shows significantly higher ratio vs other subtypes
  • Significant in both distal and middle sweeps
  • Visual US Image Findings
  • Type 2: impaired wall distensibility + complete loss of contraction
  • Type 3: both contractility AND distensibility observable
2.2 4D HRM Metrics vs FLIP Outcomes (n=123)
  • Cohort Characteristics
  • Mean age: 59 years
  • 60% female
  • Primary indication: dysphagia (89%)
  • Outcome heterogeneity reflects inconclusive EGJOO population
  • 4D EGJ Opening Metrics vs FLIP Classes
  • Both 4D EGJ DI and 4D max EGJ diameter showed significant differences across FLIP EGJ opening classes
  • Normal and borderline groups had greater values vs reduced group (p<0.05)
  • Normal and borderline groups did not differ from each other
  • 4D Clearance Ratio vs TBE Classes
  • Significant differences observed across TBE outcomes
  • TBE negative and intermediate groups had increased clearance ratios vs TBE positive patients
  • No difference between negative and intermediate groups
  • Combined FLIP + TBE Outcome Analysis
  • All three 4D metrics significantly differed across combined outcomes
  • Both negative and intermediate showed increased EGJ opening + clearance vs positive class
  • Extreme Outcome Separation
  • Zero patients with 4D EGJ max diameter <8 mm fell in negative FLIP+TBE class
  • Zero patients with 4D EGJ DI >2.1 AND diameter >10.1 fell in positive class
  • These results suggest reliable rule-in/rule-out at extremes
  • Intermediate Class Analysis
  • With abnormal EGJ metrics (diameter <8): 8/14 also had abnormal clearance ratio
  • With normal EGJ metrics (DI >2.1, diameter >10.1): 9/11 had normal clearance ratio
  • Multi-parameter approach enhances evaluation even in intermediate class
3. Histopathology-Based Achalasia Subtyping - Shieh
  • Study Population
  • n=100 patients with achalasia undergoing POEM
  • LES samples collected during surgery → paraffin-embedded specimens
  • Histopathological Parameters Assessed
  • Neural: total neurons, nitrergic neurons, glial cells
  • Immune: total immune cells, B cells, T cells, macrophages, mast cells, eosinophils
  • Fibrosis grade (via muscle staining)
  • Four Distinct Subtypes Identified via Hierarchical Clustering
3.1 Subtype 1: Classic Neuron Loss (n=59)
  • Prominent reduction of total and nitrergic neurons
  • Higher integrated relaxation pressure (IRP)
  • Represents classic neuron loss subtype
3.2 Subtype 2: Neuropreserved Injury (n=8)
  • Significantly higher total neuron counts
  • Lower IRP
  • Similar immune cell infiltration to Subtype 1 despite different neuron preservation
  • Suggests pathogenic mechanisms beyond immune-mediated injury
3.3 Subtype 3: Immune-Mediated (n=4)
  • Significantly higher immune cells (T cells, B cells, macrophages) AND nitrergic neuron counts
  • Resembles "immune height by neuron preserve phenotype" (Group A from prior research)
  • Possibly represents slowly progressive pattern
3.4 Subtype 4: Coagulation/Vascular-Related (n=29)
  • Significantly higher thrombus burden
  • Higher IRP + lower total/nitrergic neuron counts
  • Points to coagulation/vascular pathway of neural injury
  • Correlations Observed
  • Subtypes 1 and 2 show negative correlation between IRP and neuronal counts
  • Both have similar immune infiltration despite different neuron preservation
  • Clinical Correlations
  • No significant differences among subtypes in:
  • Age, gender, BMI, disease duration
  • Eckardt score, Chicago Classification
  • Other manometric parameters
  • Myotomy length or recurrence rates
4. AI Applications in Esophageal Motility Classification - Fass
  • Background
  • FLIP panometry classification recently refined to version 2.0 (Dallas consensus)
  • Prior CNN-based AI models relied on previous v1.0 iteration
  • Inter-rater reliability among specialists for FLIP interpretation is only moderate-to-good
  • Transformer Architecture Advantages
  • Self-attention mechanism allows focus on relevant input data parts
  • Unlike CNNs (local feature extraction), transformers capture spatial AND temporal dependencies
  • Well-suited for longitudinal physiologic data like FLIP
  • Novel Cost-Sensitive Penalty Matrix
  • Zero cost for correct classifications
  • 100 cost units for critical misclassifications
  • 10-50 cost units for diagnostically adjacent errors
  • Penalizes errors proportional to impact on patient management
  • Model Training & Evaluation
  • FLIP panometry v2.0 labels for model training
  • Manometry + TBE-derived reference labels for evaluation
  • Clinically pragmatic scheme: normal, abnormal, achalasia
  • Study Cohort (n=984)
  • Mean age: 53 years
  • 59% female
  • Distribution:
  • Normal manometry: 36%
  • Abnormal manometry: 25%
  • Achalasia manometry: 39%
  • Model Performance
  • Overall accuracy: 89% (3-class)
  • Binary accuracy: 94%
  • Normal: sensitivity 89%, specificity 97%
  • Abnormal: sensitivity 85%, specificity 91%
  • Achalasia: sensitivity 93%, specificity 96%
  • No achalasia patients classified as normal
  • No normal patients classified as achalasia
  • Cost Matrix Impact
  • Did not improve prediction for normal
  • Improved predicting abnormal and achalasia
  • Eliminated all high-priority misclassifications
  • Reduced moderate misclassifications
  • Remaining errors had cost penalty ≤25
  • Comparison to Prior CNN Models
  • Binary accuracy improved substantially
  • Three-class accuracy: 89%
  • Clinical Implications
  • FLIP v2.0 normal has 99% NPV for achalasia/conclusive EGJOO
  • Non-spastic obstruction on FLIP has 91% PPV for achalasia/conclusive EGJOO
  • AI model performance matches expert human concordance rates
5. Predictors of Pneumatic Dilation Durability - Samvarnarapanesh
5.1 Study Design
  • Single center cohort (2020-2025)
  • Primary achalasia patients undergoing endoscopic PD with 30mm balloon
  • Post-treatment HRM at 1 month + post-treatment TBE
  • Follow-up: Eckardt score every 3 months, TBE annually or when score ≥3
  • Reintervention criteria: Eckardt ≥3 + TBE retention
5.2 Results
  • 52 patients underwent 30mm PD → 46 completed 5-year follow-up
  • 5-year remission rate: ~59%
  • Reintervention required: ~41%
5.3 Pre-Treatment HRM Parameters
  • Resting LES pressure trended higher in reintervention group (not significant)
  • IRP during rapid drink challenge (IRP-RDC) significantly lower in remission group
  • No difference in PEP between groups
5.4 Post-Treatment Findings
  • ADA features (adequately treated achalasia): 56% in remission vs 16% in reintervention group
  • Peristaltic reappearance higher in remission (not statistically significant)
  • IRP-RDC significantly lower in remission group
5.5 Cox Proportional Hazard Analysis
  • Multivariate analysis: only IRP-RDC remained significant predictor
  • High IRP-RDC, high median IRP upright = factors associated with reintervention
5.6 ROC Analysis for IRP-RDC
  • AUC: 0.87
  • Cutoff ≥11 mmHg strongly predicts reintervention at 5 years
  • Hazard ratio: 12.6
  • Accuracy: ~81%
5.7 Survival Analysis
  • IRP-RDC <11: 87% reintervention-free at 5 years
  • IRP-RDC ≥11: Median time to reintervention = 9 months
  • ATA (adequate treatment) vs TAOO comparison:
  • ATA: 83% reintervention-free at 5 years
  • TAOO: Median time to reintervention = 13 months
5.8 Key Findings
  • Post-PD IRP-RDC is best performing physiologic predictor among post-treatment metrics
  • Cutoff of 11 mmHg provides excellent discrimination
  • Clearly separates long-term durability trajectories
6. Abdominal Compression and Esophageal Contractile Reserve - Khan
6.1 Study Design
  • 23 healthy participants: 15 younger, 8 older than 70 years
  • All asymptomatic with normal single swallows confirmed
  • Studies performed in supine position
6.2 Testing Protocol
  • Single swallows: 5 mL × 6 at 30-second intervals
  • MRS (Multiple Rapid Swallows): 5 × 2 mL at 2-3 second intervals, performed 3 times
  • All swallows performed with and without 150 mmHg abdominal compression
  • Compression delivered via non-stretchable belt + blood pressure cuff system
6.3 Contractile Reserve Definition
  • Ratio of mean MRS DCI to single swallow DCI
  • Values >1 = presence of reserve
6.4 Results: Single Swallow DCI
  • Abdominal compression significantly increased DCI in both age groups
  • Effect present in participants <70 and >70 years
6.5 Results: MRS DCI
  • Abdominal compression significantly increased MRS-induced DCI in <70 group
  • Effect did NOT reach statistical significance in >70 group
6.6 Age-Related Comparisons
  • Single swallow DCI: numerically lower in >70, but not statistically different
  • MRS-induced DCI (with/without compression): significantly lower in >70 vs <70
6.7 Contractile Reserve Enhancement
  • Without compression: 9/15 younger had positive MRS; 3/8 older had positive MRS
  • With compression:
  • Younger: 12/15 had positive MRS (3 additional participants)
  • Older: no additional participants qualified
6.8 Proposed Reserve Categories
  • Reserve 1: Contraction elicited by MRS beyond single swallow (traditional measure)
  • Reserve 2: Contraction from two challenges (MRS + compression) above single swallow
  • Reserve 3: Ability to generate contraction above MRS alone
6.9 Reserve Distribution Findings
  • Younger group:
  • Reserve 1: 9/15
  • Additional via two challenges (Reserve 2): +3 participants
  • Reserve 3: 14/15 could generate contraction beyond MRS alone
  • Older group: Dampened response to additional challenge (5/8 had lower DCI than single swallow)
6.10 Conclusions
  • External abdominal compression significantly increases esophageal smooth muscle DCI during:
  • Single swallows (both age groups)
  • MRS (younger only, not older)
  • Addition of compression during MRS elicits additional contractile reserve beyond MRS alone
  • Age negatively affects esophageal contractile reserve
7. Neuromodulator Therapy for Non-Obstructive Dysphagia - Jenkins
7.1 Background: Gut-Brain Axis in Esophageal Symptoms
  • Patients with persistent symptoms despite normal endoscopy, pH-impedance, manometry
  • Symptom severity often disproportionate to measurable pathology
  • Gut-brain axis encompasses: hypersensitivity, central processing, anxiety, hypervigilance
7.2 Esophageal Hypervigilance and Anxiety Scale (EHAS)
  • Validated patient-reported outcome measuring symptom-specific anxiety + hypervigilance
  • Original: 15 questions → Short form: 7 items
  • Two subscores: anxiety subscale, hypervigilance subscale
  • Quantifies central symptom processing
7.3 Study Design
  • Single center longitudinal exploratory cohort (Brigham & Women's Hospital, Boston)
  • Adults undergoing esophageal function testing for symptoms
  • All underwent HRM + 24-hour pH-impedance off PPI therapy
  • Excluded: prior foregut surgery, achalasia spectrum disorders
  • Patient outcomes collected ≥6 months after initial testing
7.4 Outcome Measures
  • BEDQ (Brief Esophageal Dysphagia Questionnaire)
  • GSS (Global Symptom Severity Survey)
  • Both validated in large multicenter cohorts
7.5 Cohort Characteristics (n=31 with complete data)
  • Predominantly female
  • Mean age: 59 years
  • Average follow-up: ~4.75 days (allowing assessment of durable response)
  • Only 26% had known anxiety diagnosis
  • 34% had known depression diagnosis
  • Prior neuromodulator use: only 6.5%
  • Relatively preserved motility and pH-impedance findings overall
  • Only 16% had hiatal hernia
7.6 Baseline Symptom Severity Correlations
  • EHAS total and EHAS anxiety showed strongest correlation with symptom burden
  • EHAS hypervigilance showed positive but lesser correlation
  • Standard motility parameters (including complete bolus transit) showed weak, non-significant relationships
7.7 Symptom Response After Neuromodulator Therapy
  • BEDQ: mean improvement of 34%
  • GSS: mean improvement of ~29% (statistically significant)
  • Major clinical response (≥50% improvement):
  • BEDQ: 48% of patients
  • GSS: 42% of patients
7.8 Predictors of Response
  • Reductions in EHAS total and EHAS anxiety significantly associated with symptom improvement
  • Same pattern observed for both BEDQ and GSS outcomes
  • EHAS hypervigilance alone did NOT significantly correlate with improvement
7.9 Responders vs Non-Responders Analysis
  • Responders showed greater improvement in EHAS total and anxiety vs non-responders
  • Separation especially clear for EHAS anxiety
  • Hypervigilance showed less consistent pattern
7.10 Multivariable Regression (Adjusted for Age, Sex, BMI, % Normal Swallows, Bolland's Clearance)
  • Change in EHAS total remained significant independent predictor of BEDQ and GSS improvement
  • Change in EHAS anxiety remained significant independent predictor of both outcomes
  • Effect persisted beyond demographic and physiologic metrics
7.11 Clinical Implications Model
  • Non-obstructive dysphagia may represent both physiologic AND gut-brain interaction disorder
  • Symptom amplification cycle: symptoms → anxiety → heightened perception → worsened burden
  • Interventions targeting this axis may help by improving symptom perception and response
7.12 Study Limitations
  • Small sample, single-center cohort (limits generalizability)
  • Observational pre-post design without placebo control
  • Potential responder bias due to modest survey response rate
  • Non-standardized neuromodulator class/dosing (though >80% on TCAs, gabapentin second most common)
  • Directionality between anxiety reduction and symptom improvement cannot be fully established
7.13 Future Directions
  • Prospective studies to clarify directionality
  • Define clinically meaningful thresholds for EHAS
  • Evaluate whether EHAS can guide individualized treatment strategies (neuromodulators + behavioral therapy)
  • External validation of findings in larger multicenter cohorts
8. Key Methodological Considerations Across Studies
8.1 FLIP vs HRM Concordance
  • Normal FLIP: 99% NPV for achalasia/conclusive EGJOO
  • Non-spastic obstruction on FLIP: 91% PPV for achalasia/conclusive EGJOO
  • AI model performance matches expert human concordance rates
8.2 Limitations of Standard Testing (per discussant Ravi Mikkam, San Diego)
  • Half of esophageal muscle mass is longitudinal muscle → not measured by standard techniques
  • Manometry only measures contraction phase of peristalsis
  • Distension during peristalsis may be the primary problem in some patients
  • Significant fibrosis at hiatus may not be picked up by standard testing
8.3 Clinical Utility Questions Raised
  • IRP-RDC cutoff ≥11 mmHg may guide individualized follow-up and retreatment timing post-PD
  • Abdominal compression as simple adjunct to improve assessment of esophageal motor function
  • EHAS may serve as practical tool for patient selection for gut-brain axis therapies
9. Summary of Diagnostic/Therapeutic Approaches
9.1 Advanced Imaging & Biomechanical Assessment
  • TNE-US provides direct measurement of wall mechanics (distensibility, contraction)
  • 4D HRM extracts biomechanical data from standard impedance manometry
  • Both methods show subtype-specific differences in achalasia
9.2 Histopathology Integration
  • Four distinct histopathological subtypes identified
  • Reflects heterogeneity in pathogenic mechanisms
  • May guide personalized treatment approaches
9.3 AI-Assisted Diagnosis
  • Transformer-based deep learning achieves 94% binary / 89% three-class accuracy
  • Cost-sensitive training reduces clinically consequential errors
  • Supports AI-assisted FLIP interpretation for quality assurance
9.4 Prognostic Indicators
  • Post-PD IRP-RDC ≥11 mmHg predicts earlier reintervention (median 9 months)
  • Post-PD IRP-RDC <11 mmHg associated with 87% reintervention-free at 5 years
9.5 Provocative Maneuvers
  • Abdominal compression + MRS reveals additional contractile reserve beyond MRS alone
  • Age negatively affects esophageal contractile reserve
9.6 Gut-Brain Axis Targeting
  • Neuromodulators reduce symptoms in non-obstructive dysphagia (~48% major response)
  • Improvement in EHAS anxiety (not hypervigilance alone) predicts clinical response
  • Non-obstructive dysphagia may require dual approach: physiologic + central processing intervention
