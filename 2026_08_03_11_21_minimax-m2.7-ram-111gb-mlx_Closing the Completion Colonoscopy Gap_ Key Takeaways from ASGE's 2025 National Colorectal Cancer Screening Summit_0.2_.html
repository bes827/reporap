Closing the Completion Colonoscopy Gap: Key Takeaways from ASGE's 2025 National Colorectal Cancer Screening Summit
  • Source: Gastrointestinal Endoscopy, Vol. ■, No. ■ : 2026
  • Topic: Colorectal Cancer Screening Disparities and Interventions
  • Author(s): Hosseini-Carroll PJ, Dominitz JA, Jacobson BC, May FP, Issaka RB, Schmitt CM, Adams TL, Boston IJ, Bucobo JC, Day L, Khaykis IB, Marino MJ, Rex DK, Sun E, Wynner JA, Christie JA
1. Colorectal Cancer Burden in the United States
  • Second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in US
  • >150,000 new cases projected annually
  • >50,000 deaths projected annually
  • CRC is largely preventable through timely screening and follow-up
2. The Completion Colonoscopy Gap
  • Fewer than 60% of patients complete colonoscopy within 1 year of abnormal stool-based test (SBT) result
  • Time gaps between abnormal SBT and completion colonoscopy → preventable/early-stage cancers undetected
2.1 Patient-Level Barriers
  • Fear of procedure
  • Lack of transportation
  • Competing priorities
  • Cultural stigma
  • Limited understanding of screening benefits
2.2 System-Level Barriers
  • Fragmented referral pathways
  • Delays in scheduling
  • Inadequate coordination across providers
  • 16.4% of patients did not get completion colonoscopy after abnormal FIT due to provider-related reasons (VA study, 2019)
  • Insurance coverage significantly influences adherence
  • Uninsured patients face significantly lower adherence to initial and follow-up testing
2.3 Completion Rates in Underserved Populations
  • National completion rate: 50%–70% within 1 year after abnormal SBT
  • At 8 FQHCs: 73%–96% referred for colonoscopy after abnormal FIT → only 18%–58% completed within 6 months
3. Racial, Ethnic, and Socioeconomic Disparities
  • Black patients → consistently higher CRC incidence and mortality vs white patients
  • American Indian/Alaska Native patients → higher incidence and mortality vs white patients
  • Hispanic patients → markedly lower screening rates
  • Sexual and gender minority populations → lower CRC screening than heterosexual/cisgender populations
  • Meta-analysis: LGBTQ+ individuals have ~half the odds of CRC screening vs heterosexual individuals
  • Low-SES communities → more likely to present with metastatic disease, less likely to undergo minimally invasive surgery
  • Rural residents → higher mortality and reduced access to specialty care
3.1 Interventions That Narrow Disparities
  • VA health system implementation → racial disparities in screening adherence greatly attenuated
  • Primary care-driven outreach → reduced Black-white and Hispanic-white screening gaps
4. ASGE CRC Screening Project Overview
  • Launched 2023 with goal of increasing CRC screening and completion colonoscopy for underserved patients
  • Foundation: patient navigation + SBT (most effective interventions for increasing adherence)
  • Project sites: Georgia and Maryland
  • Participants receive free screening and completion colonoscopies
  • Patient navigation professionals guide patients through full continuum of care
4.1 Partner Organizations
  • Georgia: Community Health Care Systems (CHCS) – FQHC serving rural/underserved populations
  • Navigator: Horizons Community Solutions
  • Maryland: MedChi Network Services (MNS) – coordinates care across diverse providers
  • Partner clinic: Menocal Family Practice
4.2 Navigation Services Provided
  • Explaining meaning of test results
  • Scheduling colonoscopies
  • Arranging transportation (including free gas cards in Georgia)
  • Ensuring bowel preparation adherence
  • Troubleshooting obstacles
  • Spanish-language support in Maryland

5.1 Georgia Successes
  • Overall CRC screening compliance ↑ nearly 4% since project began
  • CHCS had not previously offered this SBT
  • "Project Champion" (licensed practical nurse) credited for success
  • Robust community outreach program
  • General surgeon used for completion colonoscopies due to gastroenterologist shortage
  • Free gas cards provided for transportation
5.2 Georgia Challenges
  • Some patients with abnormal SBT hesitant for completion colonoscopy due to:
  • Fear of procedure
  • Competing life responsibilities
  • Mistrust of health care system
  • Transportation barriers
  • Mail kit delays
5.3 Georgia Advocacy Goals
  • Secure sustainable state funding for uninsured CRC screening and completion colonoscopies
  • Stress that preventative care saves lives → sound investment vs treating advanced CRC

6.1 Maryland Context
  • Lower proportion of uninsured due to Medicaid expansion
  • Focus on reaching undocumented patients
  • Average patient age: 49.4 years (younger population)
  • Small study group → no abnormal results at Summit time
6.2 Maryland Adaptations
  • Part-time Spanish-speaking staff hired for Menocal practice support
  • MNS navigators help patients:
  • Understand test results
  • Schedule appointments
  • Overcome logistical barriers (transportation, childcare)
6.3 Maryland Advocacy Goals
  • Encourage lawmakers to continue budgeting tobacco settlement funds for CRC screening
7. Summit Policy Priorities (Survey Results, n=33)
  • Rank 1: Advocate for sustainable CRC screening/completion colonoscopy funding in every state
  • Rank 2: Advocate for HEDIS measure for completion colonoscopies
  • Rank 3: Advocate for use of standard existing codes (SNOMED and/or LOINC) in HEDIS measure
  • Rank 4: Support preparation of HEDIS measure, including funding for implementation (identify best ICD-10 codes)
  • Rank 5: Advocate for CMS coverage for navigation from abnormal SBT through colonoscopy completion
7.1 Why HEDIS Measure Matters
  • Current HEDIS measure for CRC screening only assesses up-to-date status
  • Does not account for completion colonoscopy after abnormal SBT
  • Current measure considers screening complete even if abnormal results not followed up
  • Need for metric that captures 2-step process of SBT + completion colonoscopy
7.2 Summit Recommendations
  • Embed colonoscopy completion rates into quality metrics for health systems and ACOs
  • Link reimbursement and performance evaluation to colonoscopy completion after abnormal SBT
  • Create incentives for systems to invest in navigation and follow-up infrastructure
8. Patient Navigation Evidence
  • Patients with abnormal SBT + navigation support are significantly more likely to:
  • Complete colonoscopy
  • Have better bowel preparation quality
  • New Hampshire community health clinic study:
  • Navigation patients → ~11x more likely to complete colonoscopy vs control
  • Navigation patients → ~6x more likely to have adequate bowel preparation vs control
8.1 Navigation as Comprehensive Approach
  • Addresses structural and patient-level barriers simultaneously
  • Builds trust
  • Corrects misinformation
  • Solves problems that could derail care
  • Summit consensus: patient navigation is the cornerstone of cancer prevention strategies
9. Barriers Addressed at Summit
  • Unexpected billing for diagnostic colonoscopy after abnormal SBT
  • Insufficient navigation capacity
  • Limited provider initiative to prioritize completion
  • System fragmentation that impedes tracking and closing the loop
9.1 Solutions Proposed
  • Weekend colonoscopy scheduling flexibility
  • Targeted outreach to marginalized groups
  • Eliminating surprise billing
  • FQHC partnerships
  • Standardized coding
  • Creating and supporting registries to track abnormal results and completion colonoscopies
  • Durable financing mechanisms
  • Aligned reimbursement interventions

13. Key Summit Takeaways Summary
13.1 The Gap
  • <60% complete colonoscopy within 1 year of abnormal SBT
  • Underserved populations have even lower completion rates
13.2 Effective Interventions
  • Patient navigation → ~11x ↑ colonoscopy completion, ~6x better bowel prep quality
  • SBT as primary screening option for those declining colonoscopy
  • Dedicated outreach programs
  • Embedded navigation in primary care workflows
13.3 Policy Changes Needed

1. Sustainable state funding for uninsured screening

2. HEDIS measure for completion colonoscopies (with standardized codes)

3. CMS coverage for navigation through colonoscopy completion

4. Reimbursement linked to completion rates

13.4 System Changes Needed
  • Expand endoscopist pool in rural areas (including mobile endoscopy units)
  • Telehealth-assisted care coordination
  • Weekend colonoscopy availability
  • Standardized tracking registries for abnormal results
13.5 Advocacy Priorities
  • Coalition-building with professional societies, community health organizations, legislators, regulators, and industry
14. Summit Outcomes
  • Genuine sense of hope, urgency, and resolve
  • Participants from across the United States
  • Common mission: reduce preventable deaths from CRC
  • Ensure every individual has access to life-saving screening regardless of background or circumstance
  • ASGE projects demonstrated great potential to increase colonoscopy completion among patients with abnormal CRC screening results
